# A Partial Guide

A Partial Guide is a text adventure of the real world, built from open data. It starts with Addiscombe, Croydon. One narrator, Guide, describes each place from the data alone, and its confusion is earned by that data. It is psychogeography done with data: a walk read through the records others keep of a place, with drift and chance in it, but the reader is never told so; the copy says plainly what it is, and the walk shows the rest. The spec lives in the project doc "Addiscombe Narrator: spec sketch".

## Hard rules

- Never invent a fact. Guide speaks only from facts a source adapter returned. Phrases around the facts are Guide's; nouns, names, numbers and dates come from facts.
- Every fact carries provenance: source, subject, observed date.
- Guide never names a source or who kept an account (no "Wikidata", no "Food Standards Agency"): Guide alludes to them only by what they do (`keepers.ts`: "those who inspect kitchens", "those who draw the wards"), or gives what they keep as hearsay (`render/hearsay.ts`: "They say…", "It is said that…", "Someone wrote down that…"), save the words on a plaque or a bench, which are read as they are ("a plaque: “…”", "a bench. On it: “…”"), never "another account", which stored prose may not say either. The sources are credited on the About page, as their licences ask, below a word of warning in the page's voice: the data may be out of date, incomplete or wrong and is not for directions or safety, and the busier places' prose was written ahead of time by an AI, Claude, from the facts alone. Prose that names one is not stored or shown (`namesNoSource`), unless the name is also one of the map's own facts there (a tram's operator).
- Never repair data. Broken values ("est Bank") are spoken as found. The one cut: a person's name, taken out of a planning description where it is fetched (`stripNames`, below).
- Never edit OpenStreetMap. Gaps stay as found.
- Never send anyone to a way into a mine. A shaft, adit, level, air shaft, mine or old mineral working the map marks (as marked, disused or abandoned; `isMineEntry` in `core/entries.ts`), another account's mine, or a borehole named for a shaft, pit, adit or level is told by kind and name alone, "Near here", never which way it lies or how far, never as the nearest name, and no way is planned to one, nor a walk started at one (`entry` on the room's thing, left out of `routeIndex`); `npm run export` refuses a world in which any place names one with a direction or a distance (`pointsToMine`); and where the map marks any, the introduction carries the page's caution (`MINES_CAUTION`): they are dangerous, and the walk reads the records, not the ground.
- Never call public Overpass, or any OSM API, from the app. Snapshots are taken by hand with `npm run snapshot`.
- Live sources are asked from the reader's browser, for the map's own places, never the reader's location, and say nothing without an answer in time: today's weather and air (`src/lib/browser/sky.ts`, Open-Meteo) and departures at the map's bus and tram stops (`src/lib/browser/transit.ts`, TfL: buses by the NaPTAN code the map gives, tram stops found by their mapped name). The map drawn with `gps` on a wide screen (MapLibre GL JS from jsDelivr, pinned; OpenFreeMap tiles) is the third, centred on where the map puts the stop. `use the camera` shows what TfL's JamCam nearest where the map puts the stop last saw (within 1.5 km; when, from the picture's Last-Modified), and `look up` tells the weather and the planets (Astronomy Engine, worked out), then the satellites above the roofs (CelesTrak's elements, kept six hours, worked out with satellite.js) and the aircraft overhead (adsb.lol, through the flights function, `functions/flights/`, deployed separately as adsb.lol lets no other site's page read it; the page is built with `FLIGHTS_URL`, and without it asks after no aircraft) (`src/lib/browser/live.ts`). Works and closures on the roads within 200 m of a stop (TfL's road disruptions, `src/lib/browser/disruptions.ts`, London's list asked once and kept ten minutes) are told as the reader arrives, each once a walk, as hearsay, their words as found, with the day they end (`render/disruptions.ts`). `news` tells up to three of the day's headlines that name the map's place, as hearsay ("It is said today, of Croydon: “…”."), from the GDELT Project through the site's own news function (`src/lib/server/news.ts`, `netlify/edge-functions/news.ts` at `/api/news`, cached a quarter of an hour at the edge so GDELT is seldom asked), which keeps the headlines alone (no outlet, no link) and leaves out those that tell of death or crime (`untroubling`); the page is built with `NEWS_URL`, and without it, or without an answer, Guide says "No news reaches me." Without an answer the camera says only "Nothing happens." `look beyond` tells what others keep that the map does not show, within 6 km of the stop, nearest first, three at most, as hearsay (`render/beyond.ts`; a store's `beyond`, such as Digital Britannia's Roman roads and sites, `SOURCES=britannia BRITANNIA_GPKG=…`, read from its GeoPackage downloaded by hand and kept in the private data repository's `britannia/`, its British National Grid taken to longitude and latitude with proj4 (`server/gpkg.ts`), or `BRITANNIA_DIR`, its GeoJSON layers; credited on About with the project its settlements came from). It is never told in a room, only when the reader looks beyond; with nothing kept near enough, Guide owns to not knowing. They work where the page is served normally, not in the claude.ai preview.
- `feedback` is asked in the page's voice, never Guide's (`adventure/feedback.ts`): a bug, an idea or a comment; whether the reader would walk again; their own words, taken as typed, never as a command and never counted; an email address if they would like a reply, which they may skip; then everything that would be sent is shown (the place and moves, the map's date, the kind of device and the browser's family, never the full browser string or where the reader is), and nothing is sent without a yes. The site's own function (`src/lib/server/feedback.ts`, `netlify/edge-functions/feedback.ts` at `/api/feedback`) takes only the offered choices, refuses another origin, drops a filled honeypot, counts each visitor's sends in memory (five an hour), and files an issue in the private data repository with `FEEDBACK_TOKEN`, a secret in Netlify's environment alone. The page is built with `FEEDBACK_URL`, and without it says feedback is not open.
- Write the failing scenario or test before the code.
- Real places come only from dated snapshots in `data/snapshots/`. Test fixtures are synthetic and must not pose as real Addiscombe data.

## Commands

- `npm test`: unit tests (Vitest), then acceptance scenarios (Cucumber).
- `npm run test:count`: counts the tests without running them, and fails on any test skipped, left to do, or run alone (`it.skip`, `.todo`, `.only`); with `TEST_COUNT_BASE` (another checkout) it fails if there are fewer unit tests or scenarios here. CI runs it against the branch a pull request would merge into, or on main against the commit before the push.
- `npm run test:acceptance`: Gherkin in `features/`. `@snapshot` scenarios run only once the snapshot file exists; `@icebox` and `@later` never run.
- `npm run lint`, `npm run check`: Prettier, ESLint, svelte-check.
- `npm run dev`: needs `SNAPSHOT_PATH` (see `.env.example`).
- `npm run narrate:prune`: drops from `NARRATION_PATH` every piece kept for facts no stop of the map has now (a name, a sale or a suppressed record since taken out, or a place since changed), over the whole map; run it after the records or the suppression list change.
- `npm run narrate`: writes prose ahead of time into `NARRATION_PATH` (needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, capped by `NARRATE_LIMIT`). The Narrate workflow runs it on GitHub by hand, with the key as a repository secret.
- `npm run smoke`: opens the exported page (`EXPORT_PATH`) in Chromium with outside requests refused, and fails unless Guide speaks and the tour begins. CI and the Deploy workflow run it.
- `npm run underland`: writes Underland's records into `RECORDS_DIR` from Underground's newest dated files (`UNDERGROUND_DATA`, default `../apartialguide-underground/data`; `server/sources/underland.ts`): each mine's plans of the ground itself as a zone (`mine plans`: how many, of what kind, for which minerals, the last year abandoned), its returns from Hunt's ledger over that ground by its name read as a key (`mine returns`), or, where no plans' ground has it, on the map's mark of its name, never standing alone; the rocks beneath as zones (`bedrock`, `superficial deposits`, holes kept); boreholes with a name standing alone. Kept in the private data repository (`records/underland/`) until BGS confirms its licence. Zones are told on coming onto them (`render/zones.ts`, `zoneLines`): a mine's ground and its returns, three mines at most a step, the rock beneath; a flood zone also on coming out.
- `npm run gaps`: every place the walk ends, by what the map has there (`core/gaps.ts`, `deadEnds`), most first; it fails if a path still ends at a square or tram platform drawn as an area. Run it on a new snapshot and look at any new kind: a way the map joins that the walk does not cross is ours to fix, a gap the map leaves stays.
- `npm run map-check`: opens the exported page wide with the network allowed and waits for the `gps` map; CI runs it without failing on it, as it depends on others' servers.
- `npm run export` refuses a world in which Guide would say undefined, null or NaN (`wordsAmiss`).
- `npm run export`: writes the whole game as one self-contained HTML page (`EXPORT_PATH`, default `build/export/addiscombe.html`) from `SNAPSHOT_PATH`, `START_AT` and any `NARRATION_PATH`. With `SITE_DIR` it writes the site instead: `index.html` carrying only the world's head, a `404.html` that answers a path the site does not have as a text adventure answers a wall (`server/notfound.ts`: "You can't go that way."), and the world in tiles about `TILE_M` metres across (default 300; `adventure/tiles.ts`, `tileWorld`), on a grid fixed to the earth, each stop named by its tile and its own name so an exit says where to find what it leads to. The page fetches the tiles as the walk reaches them (`tiledWorld`), opening before each command the places it `needs` and fetching the way ahead while the reader reads, so it opens at once however large the map. `SITE_DIR=… npm run smoke` serves the site over http and checks it plays; the single page still plays in the claude.ai preview.
- `BBOX=minLon,minLat,maxLon,maxLat npm run snapshot` (or `AROUND_WAY=<id> RADIUS_M=<m>`): writes `data/snapshots/addiscombe-<date>.json`. Editing `data/snapshots/request.env` and pushing runs the Snapshot workflow, which takes one on GitHub and commits it. `SNAPSHOT_NAME` names the file (default `addiscombe`; records, history and Wikidata take the box of that name's newest), and `SNAPSHOT_GZIP=1` writes it as `.json.gz` (`server/json.ts`), as a whole borough raw is more than a repository takes in one file; every reader takes either. `FROM_OPL=<file> BBOX=… SNAPSHOT_DATE=…` reads a downloaded extract instead (Geofabrik's, cut to the box and written as OPL by osmium; `server/opl.ts`), the way to take a whole borough without asking a shared server for it; it keeps the areas drawn as multipolygon relations too, and no other relation. The borough's snapshots are kept in the private repository `Benedict/apartialguide-data` (`snapshots/`, with the letters to organisations in `letters/`), not in this one's history: the workflows that need them (Deploy, Narrate, Records, History, Wikidata, Snapshot) bring them into `data/snapshots/` with the composite action `.github/actions/private-data` and the secret `DATA_TOKEN`, a private snapshot taking the place of a public one of the same name; without the token, as on a fork, the public ones are used. The Snapshot workflow commits new snapshots there. About says the map's snapshot is available on request, as the ODbL asks. With `EXTRACT=<urls of .osm.pbf files>` in the request (space-separated, quoted: a box over a county line needs both counties' extracts, as Croydon's needs London's and Surrey's), the Snapshot workflow downloads and merges them and cuts the box itself (`osmium extract --strategy smart`, so each multipolygon is kept whole), as this session's network may not reach Geofabrik; a dated file with the same `SNAPSHOT_DATE` and box writes the same snapshot again, its other elements unchanged. `AROUND_PLACE=<name> RADIUS_M=<m>` takes the box around the map's own point for a place (OSM has no outline for Addiscombe), and `AREA=<name pattern>` the box around matching boundaries; both fetch from Overpass, as a whole area is beyond the OSM API's limit. With `AS_OF=<dates>` it fetches past maps from Overpass into `data/history/`, by default for the newest snapshot's box; editing `data/history/request.env` runs the History workflow. The export carries the world packed (`adventure/packed.ts`: each thing once however many stops see it, paragraphs left to the parts that spell them out), gzipped, and opened in the browser after Guide has begun: the page carries the introduction's ledger apart (`introduction`), so Guide speaks at once, and offers the tour (`offer`) once the world is open and today's weather has come or not.
- The site (`SITE_DIR`) is the microsite at its root (`server/landing.ts`: what the piece is, the tour's first place as it plays, built at noon on the map's date, the ledger's figures, what's new (the latest three days of `CHANGELOG.md`'s lines for walkers, `server/changes.ts`), where `SIGNUP_URL` is set the mailing list ("Hear what's new": a Substack publication's form opens in place only once pressed, `server/signup.ts` and `browser/landing.ts` `openInPlace`, the policy's `frame-src` naming it alone, so nothing is sent to Substack before; any other list is linked), things to try, and "Begin the walk"), and the walk at `walk/`. `GA_MEASUREMENT_ID` (a repository variable) adds Google Analytics to both, counted only once a reader says yes (`browser/analytics.ts`: a strip asks once, Settings can change the answer; nothing of Google's loads before a yes), and then only the page, the kind of each command (`parse(text).verb`) and distance marks, never what is typed; without it, nothing is sent and the About page says so. The single page has no analytics. "Remove something" (`remove/`, `server/remove.ts`) says what the walk uses, where it comes from and how a resident asks for something to be taken out: by email to `REMOVAL_EMAIL` (a repository variable), else `CONTACT_EMAIL`, with the street, the place or record and what to take out, no proof asked and no form kept, acted on within 7 days; it is linked from the front page's footer, About, the privacy page and the 404, and `help` says the same in the page's voice (`removalHint`, the engine's `removal`). The site's privacy page (`privacy/`, `server/privacy.ts`) says what stays in the browser, what is sent and to whom, and is linked from the front page, About and the consent strip; it tells of analytics and the mailing list only where they are set, and of a contact with `CONTACT_EMAIL` (a repository variable).
- The walk is the whole of Croydon (`data/snapshots/croydon-*.json.gz`, taken from Geofabrik's extract), starting in Addiscombe, and is enriched area by area: `data/areas.json` groups the borough's 28 wards into areas by the places their names share, in the order they are enriched. Only the areas marked `open` in `data/areas.json` are walked, once any is (all twenty, the whole borough, since November 2026): the rest are left out, uncompiled (`compileWorld`'s `include`), and each way into them is kept, marked with the area's name (`adventure/open.ts`, `closeAreas`; `release.ts`, `closedAreaOf`), so the walk stops there: Guide says only "The map I carry stops there.", the page's quieter note names the area not open yet, and, where `SIGNUP_URL` (a repository variable) is set, links to hear when it opens; drift, routes and tiles go no further, and the ledger is counted again from what is open. Each area has a door (`doors` in the world's head, `doorsFor`): an open area's first place is the one its `start` names (Shirley Windmill for Shirley, East Croydon for Fairfield, Woodside Green, Selhurst, Stanley Halls for South Norwood, Park Hill Recreation Ground, Broad Green, Bensham Manor School, Thornton Heath Leisure Centre, South Croydon, Waddon Ponds, Westow Park, Norbury Library, Selsdon Library, Sanderstead Pond, New Addington, Purley, Kenley, Coulsdon Library), or the tour's start (the Deploy workflow, with `DOORS_REQUIRED`, refuses a borough in which an open area's start is not found inside it); `start in Shirley` begins a walk there afresh, and the site's front page offers the open areas, and `walk/#shirley` (an area's slug) or `walk/#at=<stop>` (a place by its name on the map) begins there (`adventure/entry.ts`, `entryFrom`); an area not open yet answers as the edge does. `start at The Alma Tavern` begins at the place where a named thing or street is found, as `route to` finds it (the page fetches the street network before opening the places a command needs, so the place is open first). `start at 51.38, -0.07` begins at the place nearest a latitude and longitude typed, if within 500 metres, else Guide owns it is beyond the map (never the reader's own position, which is never asked; what is typed is never counted). `start at CR0 7HE` begins at a postcode's place, from the page alone: the world's head carries the postcodes the map's own addresses give (`adventure/postcodes.ts`, `postcodesFrom`: the middle of each postcode's addresses, given the nearest open place within 300 metres), so a typed postcode, perhaps the reader's home, is asked of no one and never counted; one the map does not carry, Guide has no place for. `start anywhere` (`somewhere`, `surprise me`) begins by chance at one of the places the world's head holds as worth beginning at (`anywhere`, `anywhereIn`: those told in prose written ahead of time, or with a name as found), chosen when the page asks what to open, so its tile is open first. An area is opened once enriched: it gains its other accounts first (`AREA=Shirley SNAPSHOT_NAME=croydon npm run records`, or both in `data/records/request.env`: the box round the area's wards, keeping only the records placed inside them, at an address the map has inside them, or about a street that runs inside them alone (`release.ts`, `keptInArea`), and asking postcode sources about the area's postcodes alone, so no other area's stops change; in files named `<prefix>.<area>-<date>.json`, which `loadRecords` joins with the rest of that source's, each record once), then prose for its busier stops (`NARRATE_AREA=Shirley`, the Narrate workflow's `area`, or `npm run narrate:prompts` with the same filter, `server/only.ts`, for prose written in a session; the area's stops are told by place before any is visited, `narrateWorld`'s `within`, so a run looks at no other stop, and the guard against repeated turns of phrase counts the area's pieces), in that order, as prose is keyed by everything known at a stop and would be lost when records arrived. The ward boundaries (`ward-*` records) cover the whole box, neighbouring boroughs' wards included. `PLACE_NAME` and `PLACE_WITHIN` name the place a site is of (Addiscombe, in Croydon, unless told); the front page gives the map's own figures as the map's, not the place's. The plan for London beyond Croydon is in the doc "A Partial Guide: London, borough by borough".
- The Deploy workflow (`deploy.yml`) builds the site from the borough snapshot (about ten minutes), the microsite and the walk in tiles, smokes it, and publishes it on every push to main (and by hand): the one place live weather can be seen, since the claude.ai preview blocks outside requests. Once Netlify is set up (the variable `NETLIFY_SITE_ID`, the secret `NETLIFY_AUTH_TOKEN`), it deploys there (`netlify.toml`; Netlify runs no build of its own): main to production, a pull request labelled `preview` to a preview of its own, named in the run's summary, and the flights function answers from the same site as an Edge Function (`netlify/edge-functions/flights.ts`, at `/api/flights`, the shared `flightsHandler`). Until then it publishes main to GitHub Pages, as before.
- `SOURCES=hygiene,… npm run records`: writes `data/records/<prefix>-<date>.json` for each source, the box being the newest snapshot's; each source's parser lives in `src/lib/server/sources/`. Editing `data/records/request.env` runs the Records workflow (as each fetching workflow runs only when the commit pushed itself edits its request, a branch that merely carries such commits, such as one joining several areas' fetches, fetches nothing again).
- The Tidy branches workflow (`tidy-branches.yml`, by hand, or by editing `data/tidy/request.env` on main, `DELETE=yes`) deletes branches already in main that GitHub does not delete itself, those merged other than by their own pull request (`scripts/tidy-branches.sh`): it lists them, and deletes them only with its `delete` box ticked; never main, a branch an open pull request is from, or one with a commit main lacks.
- `BBOX=… npm run wikidata`: writes `data/wikidata/addiscombe-<date>.json`, the items the snapshot links to and those Wikidata places in the box. Editing `data/wikidata/request.env` runs the Wikidata workflow. It is never called from the app.

## Layout

The core in `src/lib/core/` is plain TypeScript with no framework imports, so Cucumber can load it directly.

- `osm.ts`: snapshot format and index; an area drawn as a multipolygon has its rings joined from its member ways (`relationRings`), a ring that does not close left out, and is told at its outer edge as a closed way is (`adapters/osm.ts`), stopped beside when named (`stops.ts`), and, a building, blocks the view (`perception.ts`).
- `adapters/`: one `SourceAdapter` per source, each returning `Fact`s (`facts.ts`). `history.ts` compares past maps (`HISTORY_DIR`) with the current one: what has gone becomes `was:` facts, an older name a `was:name` fact. `wikidata.ts` is another account (`WIKIDATA_DIR`): facts about what the map links to, who a street is named for, and things only Wikidata places here; Guide never gives them as the map's. `records.ts` turns other sources' records (`RECORDS_DIR`) into facts marked `elsewhere`, attached to the thing the map links them to, at their address (an address two places share, such as a High Street in two parts of the borough, going to the one nearest where the record lies, and to neither when it gives no place), or of their name close by, else standing alone; `render/records.ts` holds what Guide says of each: short sayings (hygiene rating, listing, plaque) in rooms, a plaque always noticed however crowded the stop (`slippage.ts`) and a stop made beside it, as beside a named place (`landmarks`), the rest (planning applications, sales, crime) on examining a thing or street. A record stands alone only with a name. A store may also carry `areas` (ONS wards, `areas.ts`): each stop is put in the ward it lies in, and the game tells a step that crosses from one ward to another, by another account. Sources: FSA food hygiene (the date it gives an establishment not yet rated, 1 January 1901, is read as no date, and a rating that is a code, such as `AwaitingInspection`, is read as its words: “awaiting inspection”), planning.data.gov.uk listed buildings and protected trees, London Planning Datahub applications, Open Plaques, HM Land Registry Price Paid, police.uk street-level crime. OpenBenches benches and their inscriptions, each going with a bench the map has within 15 m (a record's `near`) or standing alone by its words. NBN Atlas wildlife sightings (latest of each kind per patch, open licences only), ended postcodes from the ONS Postcode Directory, and companies on the Companies House register by address. Environment Agency flood zones and recorded floods, as overlay `areas`: the engine says when a step enters or leaves one (none lie in Addiscombe's box). Two sources need keys as repository secrets, and fetch nothing without them: energy certificates (`EPC_API_KEY`, the bearer token of the register's new search, get-energy-performance-data), the latest for each address, saying how a house is rated and when, on examining it (the search gives no construction age band, so a house's age is not said); and Geograph photographs (`GEOGRAPH_API_KEY`), the latest in each patch of about 100 m, standing alone by their titles. Both are kept out of prose written ahead of time (`keptOutOfProse` in `keepers.ts`): their records reach neither the prompt nor its key, nor make a stop busier, so fetching them changes no place's prose; the template tells them. Personal detail the walk does not need is taken out where it is fetched, so it never reaches the prompt, the narration kept in the repository, or the page (`adapters/personal.ts`): a planning record keeps only its reference, address, description, decision and dates (`planningRecords`, and `ALLOWED`, the allow-list the adapter applies to its claims), and a title and the name after it, or a name after "Applicant", is taken out of the description with the word that joined it ("Rear extension for Mrs A Jones" → "Rear extension"; `stripNames`), each cut logged by reference alone, never the name, as the Records workflow's log is public; house sales are kept only as each street's range (`priceRecords`: no sale at an address, no house number, no price as paid; sales since 1 January six years before the fetch, leaving out any beyond ten times the street's median either way (a block sold whole, a garage), at least five or nothing, the range their middle 80%, the 10th to the 90th percentile, so neither end is any one sale, each end rounded to the nearest £25,000 and never to nothing), told on examining the street ("Most houses here have sold for between £400,000 and £550,000 since 2020."), never in a prompt; and a company registered anywhere but at a shop, office or other premises the map has is not told (one at a home is usually its director's). A test holds the records in `data/records/` to it. What a resident asks to have taken out goes on the suppression list, `data/suppressed.json` (`core/suppression.ts`; `SUPPRESSION_PATH`, else that file): an OSM element (`node/123`, its tags dropped so its place stays), a record by source and id (a planning reference, an FSA establishment id), or an address, for one source or all; `loadNarrator` applies it to the map, its past maps and every source's records in every build, after fetching and before narration, so it stays out after any refresh. Then `npm run narrate:prune` drops every piece of prose kept for facts no stop has now (`pruneNarration`), over the whole map, and `npm run narrate` writes those places again. Requests are acted on within 7 days, as "Remove something" promises. `data/records/use.env` names fetched sources the walk leaves out (`LEAVE_OUT=`); everything else in the folder is used, by the Pages and Narrate workflows alike. GLA London Datastore public realm trees, counted per street (in Addiscombe only TfL's highway trees are listed; the council's are not). `tfl.ts` reads TfL's arrivals as facts, `render/departures.ts` says the soonest three, headed by the way the stop serves as TfL gives it ("Parkview Road, towards East Croydon Or West Croydon"), else its letter (TfL's, or the map's `local_ref` when nothing is due), so two stops of one name either side of a road are told apart. `weather.ts` reads today's weather, as the reader's browser fetched it; it is outside perception, and no past day's weather is ever spoken. `heavens.ts` works out where the sun and moon stand now over the map's place (`astronomy.ts`, after SunCalc); calculated in the browser, it needs no answer, and like the weather is only ever for now. A street's lighting is said by the hour the same way (`render/lighting.ts`), with licence Benedict allows: a street the map says is lit has street lamps along it, and the sun now says whether they are out, coming on or lighting the way ("Street lamps light the way."); an unlit street has none. The licence is in the hour only, never in a lamp's being at a spot (the map has 8 lamps of its own; they are "a street lamp"). The template marks the sentence (`lit` on its part) and the engine says it on arrival and on `look`, two ways each, while the prompt keeps the map's "Lit at night".
- `stops.ts`: street graph to stops, where a walker would stop: where streets and paths meet, where one ends, where a street changes its name, beside named places along a street (those within 60 m of each other sharing one), and on a long street at most `spacing` metres apart (`STOP_SPACING_M`, default 150). Trunk roads are walked, as a London A-road's pavements are, and a tram stop's platform drawn as a line (`tram=yes`), so the footways meeting it from either side join, and a square or tram platform drawn as an area is crossed by a place at its centre that each path meeting its edge joins (`isCrossable`, `cross`; "an unnamed square" where it has no name), where a path comes onto one is a stop, and the platform is called by the tram stop the map names within 30 m ("the platform of Addiscombe tram stop", `platformName` in the template); only motorways, a station's platforms and a way the map closes to those on foot (`foot=no`) are not (`isWalkable`). Driveways, parking aisles, pavements and crossings make no stop (`isMinor`), nor does an unnamed way that leads a few metres to nowhere, nor an unnamed service road that leads nowhere at all; a junction drawn as several nodes within 15 m is one stop. Where pavements and crossings alone join one part of the network to another (across a main road no one walks along), the shortest such way is kept (`bridge`), so no street is cut off; a gap the map leaves stays. A stop along a street is named by its way and the nodes its stretch runs between (`w<way>.<from>-<to>.<n>`), never by how many stretches came before, so a street added elsewhere renames none. Each stop takes in its `stretch`, its half of each street to the next stops, and `narrator.ts` perceives from all of it. Exits named by compass point; a direction goes only the way it names (`s` south, `se` south-east), never a way near it.
- `grid.ts`: things filed by cell (`Grid`, `boxOf`), so what is near is looked up, never sought through the whole borough: perception's facts and buildings, the nearest name at a quiet stop, and the place nearest each postcode, each taken in the map's own order so the result is the same as through them all (the export of the borough is checked byte for byte against the one before).
- `perception.ts`: facts within reach, occluded by mapped buildings, each thing measured once however many facts it has. `narrator.ts` tells something addressed to a street that does not meet the stop only within 12 m, and the template tells things in the order they lie along the street they are addressed to, its house numbers rising.
- `tensions.ts`: conditions in the data (fragment, dated, gone, renamed, sparse, dense).
- `slippage.ts`: tensions to a `NarrationPlan` of beats and effects.
- `scene.ts`: places a plan in the network: streets here, what lies either side, where each exit leads.
- `render/`: `Renderer` port, scene to `Room` (title, paragraphs, things to examine). `AdventureRenderer` is the template, writing each paragraph also as labelled `parts` (where, street, setting, thing, nearest, frame, exits) so the engine can leave out what the reader heard at the last two places (framing lines included), say "Further along" on the same street, and turn its framing lines and its three forms of the opening (`whereForms`), never the same form twice in a row. Beyond a thing's kind and name it says, each as found (`beyondKind`), the words on it, a mapper's note (whole or not at all: none that carries a technical value or runs past 150 characters), its other names, a post box's cypher (never the reign it stands for), its date and listing, a tree's species, what a ground is for, a public footpath and its reference, a bridge or tunnel, steps counted, a board's subject, a work of art's kind, and the counties either side of a boundary marker; these reach the prompt too, through `mapWords`. It counts plain things of one kind ("Two buildings: …"), gives a house its kind and a building its storeys where the map does, leaves the usual surface unsaid in a room (asphalt, on nearly every street; on examining a street it is "tarmac", the word a walker here would use), telling only one out of the ordinary ("Gravel underfoot."), says how far each exit runs to the next street, and at a quiet stop (things in view, none named; never an empty one) the nearest named thing within 400 m (`Scene.nearest`); `CachedRenderer` uses prose written ahead of time, keyed by `promptKey(buildPrompt(scene))`, and falls back to the template. `prompt.ts` holds Guide's voice and the reference page: five writers, each with one part (Wainwright the route, Defoe the record, Sinclair the layers, Auster chance, Blake the cadence), with at most one sentence a place reaching for meaning, only with the facts' nouns; the model gets the map in words (`mapWords`, `plainKind`), never raw tags; bump `PROMPT_VERSION` when it changes.
- `narration.ts`: `narrateWorld` writes prose ahead of time through a `ProseModel` port; prose that repairs a broken name is not stored, nor prose that says what its facts do not (`render/founded.ts`, `unfounded`: a number, a quotation or a capitalised word inside a sentence that the stop's facts, as words, do not hold), nor prose that reuses a run of six words of Guide's own already in two stored pieces for the current stops (the facts' own words may recur; pieces kept for stops the map no longer has do not count). `NARRATE_ONLY=busier` narrates only stops whose data has something going on (`busier.ts`: a named thing, another account, something gone, a broken name, but not the park, golf course or wood a stop lies in or beside, nor others' accounts of it, which alone would have every path through it told the same again, nor an ended postcode on its own, which would have a quiet street's every stop told at length for one line of hearsay); the template carries the rest. Each stop is written with the prose already written for the stops one step away, so neighbours do not repeat each other; that context is not part of the key. `src/lib/server/anthropic.ts` is the Claude adapter; `src/lib/server/written.ts` reads prose written in a Claude Code session instead (`npm run narrate:prompts` writes the next stops' prompts to `build/narrate/prompts.md`; the prose goes into a JSON file of key to prose, and `PROSE_FROM=… npm run narrate` puts it through the same guards), so a Max subscription can narrate without an API key, and anyone can write a place (`docs/writing-prose.md`; `PROSE_BY` keeps "written by" a name with each piece, and About credits every writer the store names, `writersOf`); `src/lib/server/openrouter.ts` asks other providers' models through OpenRouter (`NARRATOR_PROVIDER=openrouter`, `NARRATOR_MODEL`, `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`; providers that keep prompts refused), and `npm run compare` (the Compare models workflow) narrates the same busier stops with several models and reports what the guards refused, storing nothing; tests use `testing/recorded.ts`.
- `narrator.ts`: wires the pipeline.
- `adventure/`: `compileWorld` narrates every stop ahead of time; `parser.ts` and `engine.ts` play it from a prompt. The game opens with Guide's introduction, an account of what Guide carries from the world's `about` ledger (named streets, places, the oldest record, how many other accounts, the wards the map runs into; every figure the data's), and offers the tour, which begins at `START_AT`, with the other ways in where the map has them (each other open area's door as a button while there are two at most, else one "Choose an area" that `areas` answers with every open area as a button and those not open yet named quietly; `start anywhere`; and, as the page's word, that `start at` takes a postcode, a street or a place; a walk kept from before is offered by its place, and by how far it went once it went anywhere). Each compiled stop keeps the broken names it speaks; Guide carries one only when the reader asks (`remember X`, `mark down X`, `note X`, `write down X`, or `take X`), never unasked, and by chance brings one carried back out of place (at least four moves on, never where it was found, once each, two a walk at most), never saying where it came from; once one has come back, `i` says Guide is still carrying it and `examine` it says only "A name, as I found it." Each stop also keeps the distinctive words in its names (`terms`: a word in three names of the map or fewer, not a generic place word); by chance, two names met in different places that share one are set side by side (“Havelock Road”. Earlier, “Havelock Hall”.), twice a walk at most, never explained.

The engine keeps classic conventions: a place in full the first time and briefly on return (`look` tells it in full; `verbose`/`brief`; `plainly` tells it again from the template alone where prose written ahead of time was told, so the reader can hear both voices at one place, and elsewhere Guide says it has told it plainly already); where a place's prose would tell again at least half the things heard at the last two places (its things as the template keys them), the engine tells that place plainly instead, leaving out what was heard, and prose it does tell counts its things as heard, as the template's do, a status line (`status()`: place, moves, metres walked), `z`, `g`, `i`, `xyzzy`, `colour`. The reader carries names, never things: `take` a broken name or a thing's name, `drop` it at another stop, where it lies until taken again; taken names may come back like fragments. `route to X` (or `way to X`, `directions to X`, `how do I get to X`) plans the shortest way along the streets to the nearest stop where X is a street or a named thing, matched by its words and the thing's kind (`adventure/route.ts`), and gives it leg by leg; until the reader arrives, or says `route off`, each stop adds the next turn ("Your way to “The Alma Tavern”: south-west (Lower Addiscombe Road)."), and a saved walk keeps it. The reader still walks every step. Now and then (after the first step, then every 8 steps) a reply carries a `hint`, the page's and not Guide's, shown quieter: a command the reader has not used yet (`gps`, `drift`, `route to`, `look up`, …), each at most once, remembered across walks in the reader's browser (`adventure/hints.ts`, the `HintKeeper` port; `browser/hints.ts`), so once every hint is given none comes of its own again; `hint` asks for one at any time, even then or with `hints off`; `hints off` and `hints on`, kept the same way. `place a glass` (`walk a circle`; `glass off` lifts it) sets a circle about 500 metres across down with its edge through where the reader stands (`adventure/circle.ts`): chance turns it, and of twelve turns from there it is set where the streets go round best (`walkable`: the points they reach from the one before within three arcs); twelve points round the curve, each the nearest stop and each once, are the way, a point the streets reach only far round passed over; each stop says the way round and how many points are done, and keeps the record (its names as found and its named things, each once, nothing new); back at the start the circle closes and the record is told, with how far the walk strayed from the curve. A saved walk keeps the circle and its record, so a page closed mid-circle goes on round it; on a map that has lost a point of it, the circle is lifted. Never named for what it is in the copy. `draw my walk` (`draw`) draws the reader's own walk as a plain line, north up, the shape true, in the page's colour, with no street, label or word from Guide about its form (`adventure/drawing.ts`), and the page offers to save it as SVG; the walk keeps its line (`trail`: where the map puts each stop come to, in order, never the reader's position) and an `id`, both kept in saves, so a walk picked up goes on with its own line, and every walk's line is kept in the reader's browser (`adventure/trails.ts`, the `TrailKeeper` port; `browser/trails.ts`, the newest fifty), so `draw all my walks` draws them together. Guide is Guide, never the author. `drift` (`wander`, `go anywhere`) is the other way of going: Guide chooses the next exit by chance, leaning towards what is odd in the data ahead (a broken name, a distinctive word met before, a place with something going on, a place not yet seen), never doubling back while there is another way, and says only which way ("You drift south-west."), never why; it is a step like any other. The engine plans from a `RouteIndex`, the street network alone, worked out from a whole world, or, on the site, fetched as `routes.json` the first time a way is asked for. The goal is distance: metres between where the map puts each stop, with marks (250 m, 500 m, 1 km, 2 km…) told once passed, and `score`. Replies may carry `actions` (the tour is a button) and `map`. Walks are saved in five slots (`save 1`–`save 5`, `restore`, offered as buttons) and keep themselves in an `auto` slot after every command, so a page closed mid-walk offers "Continue the walk"; `adventure/saves.ts` holds the plain-data `SavedWalk` and the `SaveStore` port, `browser/saves.ts` the browser's keeper. Everything a walk remembers is one `Walk` (`adventure/walk.ts`), with exactly the parts of a kept walk (`WalkState`): each part is kept and picked up through a table the compiler holds complete (`KEEP`, `PICK_UP`), so a part added to the walk cannot be left out of a save; what is deliberately not kept (the form of the last opening, the disruptions told, today's weather) stays apart in the engine. A walk is picked up on any map that still holds the place it stood, as stops are named by the map's own ways and nodes (on the site, by a tile on a grid fixed to the earth and that name, `tileWorld`), so a newer snapshot or a closed area does not lose it; where the place has gone, Guide says so and offers the tour; weather is never saved, as it is only ever for now.

The page looks like a classic adventure: one monospace typeface (Commit Mono), light on dark, an inverse status bar on top (place; `Walked:` and `Moves:`). The web reader is SvelteKit (`src/routes/`): a text adventure with a `>` prompt, playing the compiled world in the browser. Guide speaks to the reader as "you". Configuration comes from environment variables only.
