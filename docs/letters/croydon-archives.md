# To Croydon Archives

**To:** Croydon Archives, at the Museum of Croydon. Check the current enquiries address on the council's archives page before sending; this draft doesn't assume one.
**Subject:** Croydon's wartime air raid records, for a free, open walk of the borough

---

Dear Archivist,

I am making _A Partial Guide_, a free, non-commercial walk of Croydon told as a text adventure: https://apartialguide.com. A narrator called Guide describes each place only from public records (the map, food hygiene ratings, listed buildings, plaques, planning applications and others), credits every source, and never adds a fact of its own. Its code and words are open: https://github.com/Benedict/osm-text-adventure.

I would like Guide to be able to say, where it happened, that a bomb fell there. I can find no open record of where bombs fell in Croydon. The one well-known map of the Blitz, Bomb Sight, covers only October 1940 to June 1941, and its terms don't allow its data to be shown in a public piece like this. Croydon's own records would be far better: the whole war, the 1944 flying bombs included, kept by the borough itself.

So I would be grateful to know:

1. **What the archives hold.** For example, Civil Defence or ARP incident records, bomb maps or registers, or lists of flying bomb incidents, for the County Borough of Croydon.
2. **Whether the locations may be used.** For each incident, only what the record gives: where it fell, when, and what kind of bomb, if recorded. Nothing about people. Guide would say only that, plainly, at the place: "A flying bomb was recorded falling here, in July 1944."
3. **On what terms.** The walk is free, carries no advertising and charges no fee. It would credit the archives on its About page in whatever words you ask for.

If the records exist only on paper, I would be glad to transcribe the locations myself, with your permission, and give the result back to the archives. If you were willing for that transcription to be published under an open licence, such as the Open Government Licence or CC BY, it would be, as far as I can tell, the first open record of where Croydon's bombs fell, free for schools, historians and anyone else to use.

I would be happy to visit, to send the finished piece, or to change how the records are described if you would prefer.

With best wishes,

Benedict
